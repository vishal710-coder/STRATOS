1. Executive Summary & Core Research Question

In distributed multi-agent systems, agents making independent decisions often suffer from coordination collapse: units fail to synchronize defensive formations, contest resources inefficiently, and leave critical infrastructure exposed. STRATOS AI was built to investigate a fundamental scientific question:

Core Research Hypothesis:
"Does real-time, structured inter-agent communication measurably improve team coordination, strategic adaptability, and combat win rates compared to independently operating agents across diverse adversarial tactics?"

To evaluate this rigorously, we developed a 2D real-time strategy simulation adhering to PettingZoo Parallel API standards, implemented a heterogeneous squad of three specialized agents (Commander, Defense Guard, Attack Striker), and trained an Actor-Critic policy using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). In a multi-seed benchmark of 150 episodes across 5 random seeds, coordinated agents achieved a 100.0% win rate compared to 66.7% for independent agents (+33.3% communication advantage), completely eliminating squad wipeouts against aggressive rush attacks (0% → 100%).

2. Theoretical Formulation: Dec-POMDP

Our strategic simulation is formally formulated as a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP) defined by the tuple ⟨ 𝒩, 𝒮, {𝒜i}, 𝒫, {ℛi}, {Ωi}, 𝒪, γ ⟩:

  • 𝒩 = {commander, defense, attack} is the set of N = 3 learning agents.
  • 𝒮 is the true global game state (all positions, health pools, crystal counts, and timers).
  • 𝒜i is the discrete action space of 7 macro-actions for each agent.
  • 𝒫(s' | s, a) is the transition dynamics governed by the simulation kinematics and combat rules.
  • ℛi is the multi-objective reward signal combining team victory and role alignment.
  • Ωi is the 49-dimensional continuous observation vector received by agent i.
  • γ = 0.99 is the discount factor for cumulative future returns.

3. Game Concept & Battlefield Mechanics

The game environment represents a top-down tactical battle on an 800 × 600 pixel coordinate plane:

  • Friendly Headquarters: Located at (80, 300) with 1,000 HP. If destroyed, friendly team loses immediately.
  • Enemy Headquarters: Located at (720, 300) with 1,000 HP. If destroyed, friendly team wins immediately.
  • Crystal Depots: Three resource nodes at North (400, 150), Center (400, 300), and South (400, 450). Each holds 500 crystals; units within 40px harvest 0.5 crystals/step.

5. System Architecture & Observation/Action Pipeline

The 49-Dimensional Continuous Observation Space

Each agent receives a dense vector normalized to [-1.0, 1.0]:

  • Channels 0–4 (Self State): (xnorm, ynorm, HP ratio, cooldown ratio, alive flag)
  • Channels 5–7 (Friendly HQ): (xnorm, ynorm, HP ratio)
  • Channels 8–10 (Enemy HQ): (xnorm, ynorm, HP ratio)
  • Channels 11–18 (2 Teammates): 2 × (Δx, Δy, HP ratio, alive flag)
  • Channels 19–30 (3 Enemies): 3 × (Δx, Δy, HP ratio, alive flag)
  • Channels 31–39 (3 Resource Nodes): 3 × (Δx, Δy, crystal ratio)
  • Channels 40–42 (Global State): Friendly crystals, enemy crystals, match step ratio
  • Channels 43–48 (Tactical Comm Bus): Directive code, threat level, target Δx, target Δy, support flag, message priority

The 7 Discrete Macro-Actions

HOLD (0) • MOVE_BASE (1) • MOVE_ENEMY_BASE (2) • MOVE_RESOURCE (3) • ATTACK_NEAREST_ENEMY (4) • DEFEND_PERIMETER (5) • TACTICAL_RETREAT (6)

6. Tactical Communication Bus & 15-Step Freshness Decay

Rather than uninterpretable continuous latent vectors, STRATOS uses a structured symbolic messaging bus:

  • Directives Supported: ENEMY_DETECTED, DEFEND_BASE, REQUEST_SUPPORT, ATTACK_TARGET, RESOURCE_LOW, RESUME_OFFENSE.
  • 15-Step Temporal Decay Filter: In early builds, emergency support requests lingered in mailboxes forever, causing agents to stay in defensive loops even after hostiles died. We implemented a temporal decay filter: messages with Age > 15 steps are automatically discarded.
  • Dynamic Command Succession: If the Commander unit is eliminated, seniority passes to the Defense Guard (then Attack Striker) to maintain squad coordination.

7. Reinforcement Learning: PPO Algorithm Details

PPO optimizes a clipped surrogate objective to avoid destructive policy steps:

L_CLIP(θ) = E_t [ min( r_t(θ) * A_t,  clip(r_t(θ), 1-ε, 1+ε) * A_t ) ]

where rt(θ) = πθ(at|st) / πθold(at|st), ε = 0.2, and At is computed via Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE-λ) with γ = 0.99 and λ = 0.95.

The total loss function minimized by Adam (lr = 3×10-4) is:

L_total = -L_CLIP(θ) + 0.5 * L_VF(φ) - 0.01 * Entropy(π_θ)

9. Real Engineering Obstacles Overcome

  1. The Stale Mailbox Defect: Emergency support alerts stayed active indefinitely; solved with the 15-step temporal freshness decay filter.
  2. Broken Command Succession: When Commander died, directives stopped updating; solved with dynamic command delegation to Defense and Attack units.
  3. False Baseline Timeout Diagnosis: 1,200-step baseline appeared to win against aggressive rushers purely by accidentally wandering away to harvest northern crystals and winning on timeout points. When units were trained to attack, they walked into open fire and died. Fixed with the perimeter guard incentive and widened threat detection.

11. Tutor Q&A Defense Matrix

Q1: "Did you just hardcode rules, or is this genuine Reinforcement Learning?"
"It is genuine Reinforcement Learning. The agents' action selection is governed by an Actor-Critic Neural Network trained via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in PyTorch. The policy receives a 49-dimensional continuous observation vector and outputs probability distributions over 7 actions. What is structured is the communication schema, which feeds directly into observation channels 43 to 48. The policy network learned through PPO updates when to react to those messages."
Q2: "How do you prove the policy actually uses the communication channels rather than ignoring them?"
"We conducted a formal Policy Sensitivity Audit on the neural network weights. By keeping physical observations (channels 0–42) completely identical and only modifying the communication channels (43–48), we observed a measurable shift in action logits (Δmax = 0.0516) and a +0.931 shift in the Critic's state-value estimation. Furthermore, in our 150-episode benchmark, zeroing out the comm channels caused a 100% squad wipeout against aggressive rushers, while active comm channels delivered a 100% win rate."
Q3: "Why PettingZoo instead of standard Gym / Gymnasium?"
"Gymnasium is designed for single-agent systems. PettingZoo is the multi-agent standard, specifically the Parallel API, which allows simultaneous agent execution, decentralized observations, and individualized reward vectors."
Q4: "Why PPO over DQN or SAC?"
"DQN struggles with non-stationary multi-agent dynamics because replay buffers contain experiences generated by earlier teammate policies. SAC is designed for continuous action spaces, whereas our macro-actions are discrete. PPO with GAE is on-policy, stable, resilient to hyperparameter variations, and avoids destructive updates via clipped surrogate objectives."
