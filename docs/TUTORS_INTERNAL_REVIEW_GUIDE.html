🎮 1. The Game Concept & Battlefield Mechanics

STRATOS is inspired by classic RTS games (like StarCraft), designed as a mathematical MARL testbed:

  • Arena Size: 800 × 600 continuous 2D coordinates with bounded edges.
  • Friendly HQ: (80, 300) with 1,000 HP. If lost, friendly team loses.
  • Enemy HQ: (720, 300) with 1,000 HP. If destroyed, friendly team wins.
  • Crystal Depots (Resources): Three contested mining nodes at North (400, 150), Center (400, 300), and South (400, 450). Units mine resources by positioning over them.
  • Squad Composition:
    • Commander (280 HP, 3.2 speed): Strategic priority issuer & resource manager.
    • Defense Guard (380 HP tank, 2.6 speed): High armor base anchor, shields the HQ.
    • Attack Striker (220 HP scout, 3.8 speed, 32 dmg): High-damage flanker & siege specialist.
  • Three Opponent Types:
    1. Aggressive Rush: Directly attacks friendly HQ with no resource harvesting.
    2. Defensive Turtle: Tightly guards the enemy base perimeter (150px radius).
    3. Resource-Focused: Mines central crystal deposits to 100 units before transitioning to an assault.

🏛️ 3. System Architecture & Information Pipeline

The 49-Dimensional Observation Vector

Every step, each agent receives a normalized vector in [-1.0, 1.0]:

  • Channels 0–4 (Self State): (xnorm, ynorm, HP ratio, cooldown ratio, alive flag)
  • Channels 5–7 (Friendly HQ): (xnorm, ynorm, HP ratio)
  • Channels 8–10 (Enemy HQ): (xnorm, ynorm, HP ratio)
  • Channels 11–18 (2 Teammates): 2 × (Δx, Δy, HP ratio, alive flag)
  • Channels 19–30 (3 Enemies): 3 × (Δx, Δy, HP ratio, alive flag)
  • Channels 31–39 (3 Resource Nodes): 3 × (Δx, Δy, crystal ratio)
  • Channels 40–42 (Global State): Friendly crystals, enemy crystals, episode step ratio
  • Channels 43–48 (Tactical Comm Bus): Directive code, threat level, target Δx, target Δy, support flag, message priority

The 7 Discrete Macro-Actions

HOLD (0) • MOVE_BASE (1) • MOVE_ENEMY_BASE (2) • MOVE_RESOURCE (3) • ATTACK_NEAREST_ENEMY (4) • DEFEND_PERIMETER (5) • TACTICAL_RETREAT (6)

📡 4. The Inter-Agent Communication Bus & 15-Step Decay

STRATOS uses a structured symbolic messaging bus rather than black-box latent vectors:

  • Messages Supported: ENEMY_DETECTED, DEFEND_BASE, REQUEST_SUPPORT, ATTACK_TARGET, RESOURCE_LOW, RESUME_OFFENSE.
  • Why Symbolic? 100% human-interpretable in real time, low bandwidth, and zero black-box obscurity.
  • 15-Step Temporal Freshness Decay: In early builds, emergency support alerts stayed in inboxes forever, locking units into defensive loops. We engineered a decay filter where messages older than 15 steps are discarded.

🛡️ 7. Tutor Q&A Defense (The Top 6 Questions & Model Answers)

Q1: "Did you just hardcode rules, or is this genuine Reinforcement Learning?"
Answer: "It is genuine Reinforcement Learning. The agents' action selection is governed by an Actor-Critic Neural Network trained via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in PyTorch. The policy receives a 49-dimensional continuous observation vector and outputs probability distributions over 7 actions. What is structured is the communication schema, which feeds directly into observation channels 43 to 48. The policy network learned through PPO updates when to react to those messages."
Q2: "How do you prove the policy actually uses the communication channels rather than ignoring them?"
Answer: "We conducted a formal Policy Sensitivity Audit on the neural network weights. By keeping physical observations (channels 0–42) completely identical and only modifying the communication channels (43–48), we observed a measurable shift in action logits (Δmax = 0.0516) and a +0.931 shift in the Critic's state-value estimation. Furthermore, in our 150-episode benchmark, zeroing out the comm channels caused a 100% squad wipeout against aggressive rushers, while active comm channels delivered a 100% win rate."
Q3: "Why PettingZoo instead of standard Gym / Gymnasium?"
Answer: "Gymnasium is designed for single-agent systems. PettingZoo is the multi-agent standard, specifically the Parallel API, which allows simultaneous agent execution, decentralized observations, and individualized reward vectors."
Q4: "Why PPO over DQN or SAC?"
Answer: "DQN struggles with non-stationary multi-agent dynamics because replay buffers contain experiences generated by earlier teammate policies. SAC is designed for continuous action spaces, whereas our macro-actions are discrete. PPO with GAE is on-policy, stable, resilient to hyperparameter variations, and avoids destructive updates via clipped surrogate objectives."
Q5: "What real engineering challenges did you face?"
Answer: "Two major bugs: First, stale inbox message persistence, where support alerts stayed active forever; we solved this by designing a 15-step temporal freshness decay filter. Second, broken command succession when the Commander died; we implemented dynamic command succession so the Defense Guard immediately assumes tactical leadership."
Q6: "What is your future roadmap?"
Answer: "Scaling training to 30,000+ timesteps to compress policy entropy, introducing Asymmetric Self-Play where opponents co-evolve, and benchmarking continuous latent communication (such as TarMAC or CommNet) against our symbolic protocol."
